# BioVeracity ports honeycomb report
Research date: 13 September 2026  
Status: proposed seed report and implementation handover. Not ingested into BioVeracity; no live functionality is asserted.  
Delivery register: [Issue #38](https://github.com/bcromer77/bioveracity/issues/38)

## Decision
Use one port project to demonstrate cross-referencing a user's documents against public environmental information. Harwich Haven provides a public demonstration of the difference between a stated restoration benefit and evidence of an achieved outcome. Rosslare provides an Irish planning-document example. Cambridge–Peterborough remains the principal outreach priority.

The commercial hypothesis is that a port's environment team or its environmental consultant will pay to reduce the time spent assembling, reconciling and citing evidence. Interest, budget and willingness to pay remain unverified.

## 1. What the DfT document establishes
The publication is a call-for-evidence response summary, updated 28 August 2026, covering a consultation held 25 March–27 June 2025. It received 65 responses; 17 port-company respondents represented 58 ports.

Respondents described grid constraints, uncertain fuel choices, planning difficulties, data silos and weak coordination. Twenty-eight of 44 respondents disagreed that collaboration was sufficient. Some expected electricity capacity requirements to grow two to tenfold; this is respondent forecasting, not a measured requirement at Harwich. Seventy-seven per cent of the 17 answering the relevant question reported additional environmental monitoring.

Government's stated preference is to address at-berth greenhouse gases through vessel fuel regulation, rather than a standalone port requirement, subject to consultation. DfT also identifies a need for more evidence on air-quality impacts.

These findings support investigating evidence coordination. They establish neither demand for BioVeracity nor a software procurement budget.

Source and locators: [DfT report](https://www.gov.uk/government/calls-for-evidence/net-zero-ports-challenges-and-opportunities/outcome/net-zero-ports-challenges-and-opportunities-summary-of-responses), Executive summary; Forecasting future energy demand; Role of ports in enabling shipping; Measuring and reducing port emissions; Next steps. This is the government position in the live publication checked for this report, not a comprehensive legal-status audit.

## 2. The honeycomb: connections to test
These are proposed evidence relationships, not claims that the underlying datasets are connected or that impacts have occurred.

| Domain | User-held evidence | Public comparison | Link and limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Energy and grid | Connection offer, demand model, berth schedules | Published network plans and port proposals | Compare assumptions and dates; national estimates cannot establish a site's capacity |
| Infrastructure and planning | Project boundary, design revision, conditions register | Application, decision and published assessments | Match project identifiers and versions before comparing |
| Air and noise | Sensor series, calibration records, operational logs | Relevant monitoring stations, meteorology and public assessments | Match units, averaging periods, wind and activity; coincidence is not source attribution |
| Water and sediment | Dredging logs, sediment chemistry, turbidity observations | Waterbody records, permits and coastal evidence | Establish pathways, tide, sample dates and sediment provenance |
| Habitats and species | Survey methods, plot data, intervention footprint | Designations, conservation objectives and suitable observations | A designation establishes context; it does not prove intervention success |
| Organisations and responsibilities | Evidence owner and review assignments | Named applicant, regulator and delivery-partner records | Track who stated, measured, reviewed or authorised each item |

The central query is: **Which passages and observations support this project claim, which need reconciliation, and what evidence is still missing?**

## 3. Bounded Harwich Haven example
Selected project document: HHA's [Copperas Bay BUDS launch statement, 23 February 2026](https://hha.co.uk/news/harwich-haven-authority-launches-project-to-restore-saltmarsh-at-rspb-nature-reserve-and-boost-coastal-resilience/). It is a published operator project statement, not an independent assessment.

HHA describes reuse of dredged sediment at Copperas Bay, identifies a planned works window of 20 February–20 March 2026 and describes an intended restoration area of just over 76.5 hectares. It identifies conservation and university involvement. These are reported plans and expected benefits; the announcement does not establish subsequent completion or measured ecological recovery.

A separate [HHA annual-report case study](https://annual-report.hha.co.uk/esg-case-study/) describes Levington on the Orwell, beginning in November 2025, and anticipated monitoring. It also mentions a subsequent Copperas Bay phase. Keep the two sites and their sediment sources distinct. Its wording about monitoring throughout 2025–2026 and a future February 2026 phase needs version/date clarification before becoming a precise chronology.

### Claim-to-source comparison
“Supported” means the specified limited proposition is supported. It does not certify overall environmental performance.

| Claim or question | Source and locator | Time/geography compatibility | Status and evidence needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| A Copperas Bay restoration project was announced | HHA launch statement: date, opening description and works-programme paragraph | Copperas Bay, Stour; announced 23 February 2026; planned works dates separate | **Supported as an operator announcement.** Completion requires works records |
| The project relates to an estuary with protected ecological interests | [Natural England SPA record](https://publications.naturalengland.org.uk/publication/6069687402102784): title, site ID UK9009121 and advice notice | Independent estuary-level context; record published 12 August 2014, objectives download listed 14 February 2019 | **Supported at estuary level.** Exact footprint overlap and current advice require the latest designated-site package |
| The announced restoration area now has improved habitat | HHA launch: area and expected benefits; compare with Natural England's conservation framework | A planned intervention and a designation are not comparable outcome measurements | **Unresolved.** Require before/after habitat area, elevation, vegetation and bird-survey data |
| Levington evidence demonstrates Copperas Bay success | HHA case study versus Copperas launch | Orwell and Stour sites; different project phases | **Unsupported transfer.** Keep separate site records; compare only shared methods where justified |
| The Levington monitoring chronology is exact | HHA case study: first paragraph and final monitoring/next-phase paragraphs | Month-level start; partly retrospective and partly prospective wording | **Potential chronology discrepancy.** Request dated monitoring plan or version history; no environmental failure inferred |
| Restoration has delivered a quantified net carbon benefit | Project narrative compared with the evidence required for a project carbon balance | No matched carbon measurements obtained | **Unresolved.** Require sediment-carbon methods, dredging/transport energy, baseline, counterfactual and uncertainty |
| DfT's national energy forecasts establish Harwich's local needs | DfT forecasting section versus a missing site demand model | National respondent sample versus a specific authority/project | **Not a valid site conclusion.** Require actual connection and demand records |

Natural England explicitly warns that the older Access to Evidence packages are not regularly updated and points to its [Designated Sites System](https://designatedsites.naturalengland.org.uk/). That system's search page was accessible; a current site-specific advice package and spatial intersection were not retrieved. This is independent designation context, not independent corroboration of restoration results.

### What to request privately
Request only documents necessary for the agreed question:
- Approved intervention boundary and separate sediment-source/disposal locations.
- Relevant licence/consent references, conditions and dated completion records.
- Pre-intervention and repeat survey data, methods, units, detection limits and sampling effort.
- Sediment volumes and chemistry, tide/weather context and placement logs.
- Monitoring responsibilities, review comments and remaining actions.
- Carbon assumptions and energy records only if a carbon claim is in scope.

The first useful comparison would join one claimed habitat benefit to the relevant survey plots and current conservation objectives. It would show what was measured, where and when, and what remains a prediction.

## 4. Is there an Irish equivalent?
The closest national counterpart located is the **draft revision of Ireland's National Ports Policy**, with an associated Strategic Environmental Assessment environmental report. It is broader than the UK net-zero response summary.

The [Public Consultation Institute of Ireland notice](https://publicconsultation.ie/revised-national-ports-policy-opens-for-public-consultation/) reports consultation dates of 3 July–28 August 2026 and links to the [Department of Transport consultation](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-transport/consultations/public-consultation-on-the-draft-revision-of-the-national-ports-policy-2013/). Those announced dates have passed. The Department page returned 403 during this review, so a later extension or final adoption was not verified.

The official [SEA environmental report](https://assets.gov.ie/static/documents/c7edfd1e/National_Ports_Policy_-_SEA_Environmental_Report.pdf) was located in search indexing, showing version F02 dated 30 June 2026 and steps towards finalisation. Full retrieval failed. Register it as located/awaiting full review, not fully read or adopted policy.

There is also a concrete EU regulatory counterpart. The Commission states that [FuelEU Maritime](https://transport.ec.europa.eu/transport-modes/maritime/decarbonising-maritime-transport-fueleu-maritime_en) has applied fully since 1 January 2025, apart from earlier monitoring-plan provisions. Its shore-power/alternative-zero-emission requirements begin in 2030 for in-scope passenger/container ships at AFIR-covered ports, with further coverage in 2035. Scope and exemptions matter. This is a prospective evidence-support context, not a claim that BioVeracity provides statutory verification.

## 5. Irish seed: Rosslare ORE Hub
The [An Coimisiún Pleanála case register](https://www.pleanala.ie/en-ie/case/323952), reference OA26.323952, records an application lodged on 10 December 2025 and lists an EIAR and NIS. It also provides a project boundary, a copy of Maritime Area Consent, a water/wastewater feasibility document, WFD assessment and specialist chapters. The page inspected did not establish a final planning decision.

Read project document: [Rosslare ORE Hub EIAR Non-Technical Summary](https://www.pleanala.ie/publicaccess/Case%20Documentation/323952/Applicant%20Documents/Application%20Docs/05.%20EIAR/Vol%201%20-%20Non-Technical%20Summary/Non-Technical%20Summary.pdf). Relevant sections were inspected, not all specialist appendices.

Its section 4.3.1, printed page 14 (PDF page 18), describes waterbodies with moderate-to-good status and some at risk. Section 4.3, printed page 16 (PDF page 20), makes residual-impact conclusions conditional on mitigation. This is a strong test of whether search preserves qualifications.

| Comparison | Evidence state | Next join |
|---|---|---|
| Summary waterbody description versus individual EPA records | **Unresolved externally:** applicant summary read; matching EPA records not yet retrieved | Resolve waterbody identifiers and assessment cycle; retain both status and risk |
| Predicted residual impacts versus mitigation delivery | **Conditional applicant assessment**, not demonstrated performance | Link every conclusion to its stated mitigation and later implementation evidence |
| Application lodged versus permission granted | **Lodgement supported; permission not established in this review** | Retrieve decision/order if subsequently published |
| Project boundary versus ecological and coastal constraints | Boundary files listed; intersection not performed | Validate coordinate system and geometry, then intersect reviewed public layers |

Recommended Irish query: **Which public records and mitigation commitments need to be checked before relying on this report's water-quality conclusion?**

This offers a practical Irish comparison alongside Harwich. It is not evidence of a defect in the Rosslare application.

## 6. One prospective paid use case
Offer one evidence review for one port project: a capped document set, an agreed source list, a cited comparison table, chronology, gaps register and one review meeting. Exclude field surveys, engineering design, statutory certification and promises of consent.

Prospective buyer: environment/sustainability manager or project director; the incumbent environmental consultant may be the delivery channel. No buyer or budget has been confirmed.

Before quoting, agree the job and manual baseline. Measure actual staff hours for document collection, comparison, citation checking, correction and approval on equivalent tasks. Include client review and BioVeracity founder labour. Measure turnaround separately from staff effort.

Illustrative internal economics only: a £1,500 review, eight delivery hours at £50 plus £100 processing/licensing cost, yields £1,000 contribution (66.7% of revenue), before acquisition, overhead and tax. At 20 hours, contribution falls to £400 (26.7%). This is a sensitivity, not a published offer or evidence of willingness to pay. Payment terms, acquisition cost, repeat frequency and specialist-review cost remain unknown.

Proposed continue rule: customer validates useful output and positive net benefit, requests another paid review, and delivery margin meets the agreed threshold. Do not scale from public project counts.

## 7. Scoped ingestion and search handover
Research is ready for review; implementation remains outstanding. Coordinate with [Issue #4](https://github.com/bcromer77/bioveracity/issues/4). Inspect the current ingestion/search code and open work before selecting implementation details; this report is not a code audit.

1. **Register sources.** Begin with DfT, HHA Copperas statement, HHA Levington case study, Natural England record, Rosslare case register and NTS. Record source URL, publisher, title, version, licence/access status, retrieval date, event/publication dates separately and readable locator. Block full-content ingestion until reuse is reviewed. Keep the Irish policy PDF in the failed-retrieval queue.
2. **Represent claims faithfully.** Store operator/applicant statements separately from regulator records and analyst inference. Preserve planned versus actual dates and mitigation qualifiers. Use stable entity IDs for HHA, Copperas Bay, Levington, the Stour/Orwell estuaries and Rosslare OA26.323952. Do not invent coordinates or geospatial cells.
3. **Link evidence.** Every proposed relationship needs the claim passage, target passage, relationship type, date/spatial compatibility and review status. Similarity can find candidates; it cannot approve the relationship.
4. **Use existing reviewed-search eligibility.** Deduplicate by source/version, retain revisions and failures, and prevent repeated imports from duplicating records. Separate historical backfill from later change detection. This handover does not activate a scheduler.
5. **Demonstrate the user journey.** Open a project passage, retrieve a relevant external record, open both original citations, save a qualified comparison and export a readable report. Confirm search includes the reviewed content and that excluded sources remain excluded.
6. **Verify meaningful failure cases.** A planned works date must not become completion; national evidence must not establish a local measurement; Levington must not merge with Copperas; a newer waterbody cycle must not silently contradict an older assessment; private documents must not appear in another case/account's search or exports.
7. **Report deployment stages separately.** Record proposed, implemented, tested, merged, deployed and verified-live status. The user owns deployment. No production changes are authorised by this research handover.
